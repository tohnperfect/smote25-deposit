\src{S}{}The screen was done in three looks by separate sessions of the AI assistant (Claude Opus 5.5), which followed a written codebook (online appendix, Section~S5). The second AI model (Claude Fable 5.2) checked it: blind to the screen's decisions, it decided a random sample of <<check_blind_inc_n>> included and <<check_blind_exc_n>> excluded records and agrees with the screen on <<check_blind_agree>> of the <<check_blind_decided>> that it included or excluded (<<check_blind_pct>>\%, Cohen's $\kappa = <<check_blind_kappa>>$), and it then decided the <<check_unsure_all>> records that the screen had left unsure. The authors settled the hard cases with four rulings. Ruling A excludes a record whose only version has been removed by its repository, because it cannot be read in full; these are deposits that Zenodo has taken down, nearly all as spam. Ruling B counts a chain of existing resampling steps as the paper's own method only if the paper presents the chain as its contribution and compares it with at least one other way of resampling. Ruling B2 never counts a standard generator used without a stated change, such as a plain GAN, as the paper's own. Ruling C keeps a record in scope whatever its task: segmentation, survival analysis or object detection, for example, count like classification. The authors applied rulings A and C themselves. The check applied B and B2 to the records that it decided; for the other included records, a further pass of AI sessions recorded from each title and abstract the facts that the two rulings turn on, and a script applied the rulings to these facts. Appendix~\ref{app:search} gives the pilot of the search string, the three looks, the check and the rulings in full, and Table~\ref{tab:aisteps} lists each step with its check and its result.
