\src{S}{}In the reference-collection tier, the ten methods of Table~\ref{tab:recommend} and the four baselines were run under the same protocol on all <<rr_tasks>> tasks of the reference collection (Appendix~\ref{app:reference}), which fall into <<rr_families>> families of the Data section; five of the methods were implemented from their papers by AI sessions (Section~\ref{sec:whichmethod}; online appendix, Section~S7). \src{A}{S}A cloud workspace (Linux, two cores) ran <<rr_tasks_cloud>> tasks with the same versions of scikit-learn and the samplers' libraries, and the Apple M5 Pro of the core tier ran promotion, shoppers, musk, and smoke detection. Mammography and ijcnn1 ran on both machines, whose processors and numerical libraries differ: <<rr_xm_pct_mammography>>\% and <<rr_xm_pct_ijcnn1>>\% of their cells differ in at least one of ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, G-mean, and the Brier score, but no mean over the folds differs by more than <<rr_xm_mean_max>>, and no difference from SMOTE with the random forest or gradient boosting by more than <<rr_xm_vs_smote_max>>; the tests use the cloud's copies. Rows with a missing value were dropped and nominal features one-hot encoded. Of its <<rr_cells>> cells, <<rr_ok>> gave a result; <<rr_error>> are sampler runs that stopped with an error, where a training fold held too few minority examples for five neighbors (SMOTE, G-SMOTE, and ADASYN) or ADASYN found no minority example with a majority neighbor or no point to generate, and <<rr_na>> are $k$-NN with class weights. SMOTE, G-SMOTE, and ADASYN could not run on ijcnn1, whose training folds hold too few minority examples, nor on some folds of one ecoli task; as in the core tier, a method's mean is taken over the folds where it ran, so the family of ijcnn1 has no mean for SMOTE, and the tests use the <<rr_test_families>> families in which SMOTE ran. With the random forest and gradient boosting, no variant differs from SMOTE by more than <<rr_var_maxabs>> in median ROC-AUC or PR-AUC, no resampling gives the lower Brier score, and a tuned threshold the higher G-mean (Section~\ref{sec:whichmethod}).
