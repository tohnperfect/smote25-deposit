\src{A}{S}Coding thousands of papers in full is beyond a survey, so the coverage of the corpus is tested on a sample. \src{S}{}From the records that pass the screen, we drew <<cov_per_period>> at random in each of four periods, <<cov_drawn>> in all: up to 2009, 2010 to 2015, 2016 to 2020, and 2021 to 2026, the periods into which the evaluation extraction groups its studies. Each drawn paper was assessed in full text under the criteria above. Each eligible paper was then coded with the fields used for the corpus. The first is its label: Core if interpolation is its main generator, Partial if interpolation is one of several generators, and Not counted if it generates in another way. The others are the decisions of the SMOTE rule that its generator changes, the class setting in which it is run (two classes, more than two, or both), and items of its evaluation, among them whether it repeats its partitions, tests significance, and releases its code. \src{A}{S}The coding was done by AI sessions under a written codebook (online appendix, Section~S5). \src{A}{S}Full texts came from open routes (arXiv, Europe PMC, and open copies that Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex point to) or, failing these, through the authors' university library; <<cov_retrieved>> of the <<cov_drawn>> drawn papers could be obtained, and the counts rest on them. \src{S}{}Separate AI sessions coded these papers a second time, blind to the first coding, an AI session settled the differences, and the authors checked a sample of the settled codes against the papers (Appendix~\ref{app:search}; Table~\ref{tab:aisteps}).
