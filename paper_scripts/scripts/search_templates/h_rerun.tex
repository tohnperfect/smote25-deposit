\src{A}{S}The rerun on the reference collection of Appendix~\ref{app:reference} applies the protocol of the independent benchmark, with the baselines of Section~\ref{sec:before}, to all ten methods (Appendix~\ref{app:benchmark}; online appendix, Section~S7). A method meets C1 when its median difference from SMOTE over families of tasks is no worse than $-$<<rr_rope>>, the region of practical equivalence of our tests, in ROC-AUC and in PR-AUC with each of the two strong learners, the random forest and gradient boosting; the rule was fixed before any result was read. Five of the ten methods come from the libraries of the benchmark. The other five had no usable public implementation, and AI sessions implemented each from its paper and validated it against a number the paper prints (Section~\ref{sec:ai}): SMOM and AB-SMOTE reproduce theirs; FW-SMOTE comes close, but its paper's gain over SMOTE appears only when its configuration is chosen on the test folds; RSMOTE and CGMOS do not reproduce their papers' results, although for RSMOTE our runs reproduce the paper's own values for SMOTE and for no resampling, and CGMOS as its paper defines it chooses its seeds almost as SMOTE does (a total-variation distance of at most 0.0025 from SMOTE's uniform choice on five datasets). We run all five as their papers describe them, and Table~\ref{tab:recommend} says which reproduced. The rerun covers all <<rr_tasks>> tasks of the collection, in <<rr_families>> families.

\src{A}{S}No variant differs from SMOTE beyond that region. With both strong learners, the median difference from SMOTE over the <<rr_test_families>> families in which SMOTE ran is at most <<rr_var_maxabs>> in ROC-AUC and in PR-AUC for every variant, and Holm-corrected Wilcoxon tests find <<rr_var_holm_sig|w>> of these <<rr_var_tests>> comparisons significant, so every variant meets C1. The methods do differ on single tasks (within a family the ten methods span a median of <<rr_range_rocauc_rf>> in ROC-AUC with the random forest, and more than <<rr_rope>> in <<rr_range_rocauc_rf_n01>> of the <<rr_test_families>> families), but no variant does better or worse than SMOTE consistently enough to move the median. \src{S}{}The variants differ from SMOTE in operating point and calibration: AB-SMOTE and FW-SMOTE, which by their papers' defaults add fewer points than SMOTE, give a lower G-mean at the default threshold and a lower Brier score, and Safe-Level-SMOTE gives a higher Brier score with the random forest (online appendix, Section~S7). \src{S}{}The baselines repeat the finding of the core tier. No resampling has the same median ROC-AUC and PR-AUC as SMOTE. A median difference of zero does not mean that the two agree in every family: in PR-AUC with gradient boosting, no resampling is lower than SMOTE by more than <<rr_rope>> in <<rr_none_prauc_gb_nworse>> of the <<rr_test_families>> families and higher by more than <<rr_rope>> in <<rr_none_prauc_gb_nbetter>>, and the Bayesian signed-rank test, which takes the size of every family's difference into account, gives a probability of <<rr_none_prauc_gb_psmote>> that SMOTE is better by more than <<rr_rope>>. No resampling also gives the lower Brier score (by <<rr_none_brier_rf_abs>> with the random forest and <<rr_none_brier_gb_abs>> with gradient boosting), and a threshold tuned on the training folds gives a higher G-mean than SMOTE (by <<rr_threshold_gmean_rf_abs>> and <<rr_threshold_gmean_gb_abs>>).

\src{A}{S}On this evidence, the ten simple methods are interchangeable in the measures that do not depend on a threshold, and none is a reason to prefer it to plain SMOTE, which remains the reference that every variant is measured against. A variant can still be chosen for its mechanism (fewer synthetic points, a different allocation or partner), but then it should be checked on the data at hand with a strong learner, a tuned threshold, and calibration reported (Section~\ref{sec:checklist}). The gains that RSMOTE and CGMOS print in their papers are not reproduced, and FW-SMOTE's only with settings chosen on test folds, so those gains should not be cited as evidence for the methods. SMOM is the only Multiclass-only method that meets C2 and C3, and the rerun, which is two-class, tests it on two classes only; the simplest alternative is not a method paper of its own: decomposing the problem one-vs-one and applying plain SMOTE to each pair of classes \citep{fernandez2013analysing}.
